# Building in public is the messy middle

Most people treat building in public like a highlight reel.

I think that misses the point.

The useful part is not the win post. It is the bit before the win, when the thing is half broken, the plan is changing, and you are still trying to make it work without pretending it is clean.

![One stream many clips](images/one_stream_many_clips.jpg)

Caption: One stream turning into a set of clips.

That is the part I want to get better at showing.

Not because every bug needs a dramatic story. Most bugs are boring. Most fixes are small. Most progress looks stupid until it works.

But that is exactly why it is worth showing.

A clean launch post tells people what happened.

The messy middle tells people how you think.

## The product is not just the final screen

When I started building Vibe Zone, the simple idea was this:

stream once, then let the system help turn that stream into clips, thumbnails, notes, and posts.

That sounds neat in one sentence.

In practice, it has been a lot of weird little problems.

Captions drift.

A clip looks good until the proof frames show something off.

A thumbnail looks clickable but says nothing.

A render works once, then breaks when the source changes.

The upload flow feels easy until it has to be safe enough to trust.

![First auto clip](images/first_auto_clip.jpg)

Caption: The first clip that started to feel real.

This is the stuff I think build in public is actually good for.

Not pretending the work is smooth.

Showing the checks. Showing the wrong turns. Showing the moment where the tool stops being an idea and starts becoming a thing you can argue with.

## Receipts beat announcements

I do not want to post endless versions of:

big update soon

almost ready

cooking something

Those can be fun, but only if there is something real behind them.

The better post is usually smaller.

Here is the clip.

Here is what broke.

Here is what changed.

Here is the next bottleneck.

That gives people something to judge.

It also keeps me honest.

![Ship live fix later](images/ship_live_fix_later.jpg)

Caption: A rough build moment becoming a finished short.

There is a weird trust that comes from showing the rough version first.

If the final version works, people saw the path.

If it fails, people saw why.

Either way, it feels more real than acting like everything appeared fully formed.

## The constraint is the whole story

I have a full time job. I have a family. I do not have infinite hours to polish everything into a fake perfect founder diary.

That constraint is not a side note.

It is the reason the product exists.

If the system only works while I sit there doing every step by hand, it is not solving the problem.

The point is to make the work easier to review, easier to trust, and easier to ship without turning the whole thing into another job.

![Full time build](images/full_time_build.jpg)

Caption: Building around real life, not around a perfect calendar.

That is why I like building this in public.

The pressure is useful.

People can see the promise.

They can also see when the promise is not true yet.

That gap is where the product gets sharper.

## What I am learning

Building in public works best when it has receipts.

A screenshot is better than a vague claim.

A broken clip with a fix is better than a polished slogan.

A tiny lesson from today is better than a giant lesson that sounds borrowed.

I am trying to make the account feel like a workbench, not a billboard.

Some days that means a finished clip.

Some days it means showing the part that made me want to throw the laptop out the window.

Both are useful.

Because the messy middle is not the stuff you hide until the product is ready.

It is the product telling you what it needs to become.
