# Dog Staring Psychology — Research Notes

Working premise: **Why your dog stares at you from across the room**.

## SOP / production guardrails carried forward
- Separate YouTube Automation project; no public posting without explicit owner approval.
- Premium original hand-drawn educational explainer style: warm cream paper texture, charcoal linework, soft watercolor grain, golden dog/beagle palette, terracotta/coral owner sweater, no readable generated text/logos/watermarks/arrows.
- 16:9 YouTube format.
- For the first ~80 seconds, create intentionally varied visual beats; avoid repeated same dog/same pose/same room frames.
- Script should be research-backed, emotionally compelling, not overclaim beyond sources.

## Sources checked

### Nagasawa et al., 2015 — Science
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25883356/
Title: “Oxytocin-gaze positive loop and the coevolution of human-dog bonds.”
Useful points:
- Human-like mutual gaze in dogs may have been acquired during domestication.
- Dog gazing behavior, but not wolf gazing, increased urinary oxytocin concentrations in owners.
- Owner affiliation then increased oxytocin in dogs.
- Nasal oxytocin increased dog gazing behavior, which increased owner urinary oxytocin.
- Good script use: a dog’s stare can be an active bonding signal, not just “wanting food.” Avoid saying every stare is love.

### Merola, Prato-Previde, Marshall-Pescini, 2012 — PLOS ONE
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23071828/
Title: “Dogs’ social referencing towards owners and strangers.”
Useful points from search/fetch:
- Dogs looked to humans when encountering a novel/potentially scary object.
- Owner emotional cues influenced dog behavior toward the object.
- Owner relationship mattered more than stranger cues.
- Good script use: dogs stare because human faces are information dashboards; they look to us to decide what a situation means.

### Horn, Huber, Range, 2013 — PLOS ONE
PLOS: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0065296
Title: “The Importance of the Secure Base Effect for Domestic Dogs – Evidence from a Manipulative Problem-Solving Task.”
Useful points:
- Dogs worked longer on a problem-solving task when their owner was present.
- Owner silent vs encouraging mattered less than owner presence.
- An unfamiliar human did not produce the same increase.
- Good script use: dogs are affected by the presence of their specific person; looking/staring can be a way to locate the secure base.

## Core caution
A stare can mean many things depending on the scene: bonding, checking cues, anticipation, request, uncertainty, stress, guarding, pain, or arousal. The script should teach viewers to read the body and the context, not translate “stare” as one fixed meaning.
