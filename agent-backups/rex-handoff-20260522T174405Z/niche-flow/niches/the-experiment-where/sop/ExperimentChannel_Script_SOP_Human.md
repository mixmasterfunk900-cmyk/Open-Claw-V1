*Reference video: "The Experiment Where Babies Were Raised With No Language" (wGu5AxYtqaQ)*

---

## CHANNEL DNA

This channel does one thing: it takes the most disturbing experiments ever conducted on human (or animal) subjects, proves they revealed something profound about what it means to be human, and ends with a single line that reframes everything the viewer thought they knew. It is not a history channel. It is not a science channel. It is a **philosophical gut-punch machine dressed in historical horror**.

The viewer stays because they think they are watching a story about a cruel experiment. They stay to the end because they realise they are watching a story about themselves.

## SERIES CONCEPT

Every video follows this exact promise to the viewer:

> *Someone did something terrible to find something out. What they found out is more disturbing than what they did.*

The title format is always:
- "The Experiment Where [subject] [disturbing past-tense verb]"
- "The Experiment Where [subject] [was done to them]"

Examples:
- *The Experiment Where Babies Were Raised With No Language*
- *The Experiment Where Children Were Taught to Fear*
- *The Experiment Where Soldiers Were Ordered to Electrocute Strangers*
- *The Experiment Where A Man Was Kept Awake For 11 Days*
- *The Experiment Where Dogs Were Given No Love*
- *The Experiment Where A Chimpanzee Was Raised As A Human*
- *The Experiment Where Prisoners Became Guards*

## HOOK PLAYBOOK

### Hook Type: Historical Act + One Chilling Rule + Forbidden Question

This is the primary hook formula for the series. Never deviate from it.

**Template:**
```
"In [specific year], [authority figure] decided to find out [fundamental question about human nature].
To do it, [they took an action involving subjects who could not consent] and gave one rule:
[the constraint]. What [happened/he found/they discovered] [pivot to darkness or result]."
```

**Reference example (verbatim from source video):**
> "In the year 1211, an emperor decided to find out what language God spoke. To do it, he took a group of newborn babies away from their mothers, handed them to silent nurses, and gave one rule: feed them, bathe them, keep them alive, but never, under any circumstances, speak a single word to them."

**Why it works:**
- The specific year creates instant credibility and historical weight
- The authority figure (emperor, scientist, government) gives it scale
- "One rule" creates a mechanical simplicity that makes the horror feel deliberate, not accidental
- The forbidden question hooks the intellect
- The constraint creates immediate dramatic tension before a single result is revealed

**Emotional trigger:** Morbid curiosity + moral vertigo (someone actually did this)

---

### Secondary Hook: The Result First

Use when the result is so shocking it IS the hook:

**Template:**
```
"[Shocking result stated plainly]. It happened in [year], when [authority figure]
tried to [the question they were asking]. Here's what they did."
```

Use this variant when the experiment is already semi-known (e.g. Milgram) and the standard formula would feel like a delayed reveal for something people half-know.

## VIDEO BLUEPRINT

### Full structure with timing (target: 7–10 minutes)

```
BLOCK 1: THE INCITING ACT (0:00–0:45)
  → The hook. State the experiment in full.
  → Give the result immediately — do NOT withhold.
     ("The babies died." Full stop. Now why?)
  → The result should land within 45 seconds.
  → End block with: the question the experiment was trying to answer.

BLOCK 2: THE FIGURE (0:45–1:30)
  → Who was behind this? Make them fascinating, not just evil.
  → One or two details that make them feel real and complex.
  → Their obsession is the engine. What did they believe?
  → Note any historical credibility issues if the source is disputed.

BLOCK 3: THE RESULTS + FIRST THESIS (1:30–2:45)
  → What actually happened in detail.
  → The first philosophical extraction — one punchy line.
     ("Silence doesn't just mute a room. It stops something growing.")
  → This is the bridge from history to science.

BLOCK 4: MODERN PARALLEL #1 (2:45–4:15)
  → First modern case study that validates and deepens the thesis.
  → Name the researcher, the year, the institution — specificity is credibility.
  → What did they find? What does it prove?
  → Raise the emotional stakes: these are real people with real suffering.

BLOCK 5: MODERN PARALLEL #2 (4:15–5:30)
  → Second case study that adds a new dimension.
  → Should complicate or deepen the thesis, not just repeat it.
  → This is where you introduce friction: what DIDN'T they expect?

BLOCK 6: THE MECHANISM (5:30–6:15)
  → Name the scientific concept at work. One term. Define it simply.
     (Critical Period / Learned Helplessness / Deindividuation)
  → Short. Clinical. This is the intellectual anchor.

BLOCK 7: THE EDGE CASE (6:15–7:15)
  → One story that breaks the assumption open further.
  → This is your most emotionally resonant case.
  → Should feel personal and specific — a name, a face, a detail.

BLOCK 8: THE CONCLUSION (7:15–8:30)
  → Return to the original experiment from Block 1.
  → The reframe: what does the original experiment actually tell us
     about human nature in 2025?
  → Final line: ONE sentence. Paradox preferred.
     Formula: "We are not [what we assumed]. We are [the disturbing truth]."
     Example: "We are not born human. We are talked into it."
```

## NICHE BEND GUIDE — DOG CHANNEL (MindfulPaws Psychology)

Every experiment topic can be bent toward dogs. This matters because your dog channel already has the audience; the new channel builds separately but feeds ideas between them.

**Bend method:** Take the human experiment's thesis → find the animal behaviour equivalent → structure identically.

**5 ready-to-use bends:**

1. *Human: Babies raised with no language* → *Dog: Dogs raised with no human voice (kennel isolation studies, cortisol research, Miklósi attachment work)*

2. *Human: Harlow wire mother (no love)* → *Dog: Shelter dogs and learned helplessness — when they stop asking to be touched*

3. *Human: Milgram obedience* → *Dog: Why your dog obeys someone it just met — dominance, learned submission, and what "good boy" actually means neurologically*

4. *Human: Stanford Prison Experiment* → *Dog: Pack hierarchy myths — what wolves actually do vs what we project onto dogs*

5. *Human: Sleep deprivation experiments* → *Dog: What happens to a dog that never gets deep sleep — the science of dog fatigue nobody talks about*

## IMPORTANT RULES

- Never summarise where you can systematise
- Always extract hook text verbatim from reference material — never paraphrase when quoting
- State the result of the experiment within the first 45 seconds — always
- The final line must be a paradox or inversion. If it's just a summary, rewrite it.
- Two modern case studies minimum per video — the historical case alone is never enough
- Name every researcher, year, and institution — vagueness kills credibility
- Never moralize. Present. The viewer forms the moral.
- The question the video answers must be universal: not "what happened to these babies" but "what does this mean about all of us"
