# Research Deep Dive — Car Payment Trap

Key mechanisms:

1. Monthly-payment framing hides total cost. Dealers/lenders can make a car feel affordable by stretching the term, but a lower monthly payment can mean more total interest and longer exposure to negative equity.
2. Transportation is often non-optional. Unlike a luxury purchase, a car can be tied to work, childcare, groceries, school, and safety. That makes the buyer emotionally vulnerable to accepting a bad structure.
3. Long terms increase fragility. A 72- or 84-month loan can outlast the buyer's job, commute, warranty, or life situation.
4. Insurance, fuel, maintenance, registration, parking, and repairs are part of the real monthly car cost, but they often enter the budget after the loan is signed.
5. Negative equity can roll one bad car decision into the next, turning transportation into a debt treadmill.
6. Younger households have fewer buffers, shorter credit histories, and more pressure from rent/student debt/cost of living, making a large fixed auto payment more dangerous.
